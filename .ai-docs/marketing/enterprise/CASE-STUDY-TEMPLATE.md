## Case Study Interview Questions

### Opening Questions (5 minutes)

1. Tell me about your company. What do you do?
2. Who are your customers?
3. What stage is the company at? (Team size, funding, growth)
4. What's your role, and how long have you been there?

### The Problem (10 minutes)

1. What infrastructure challenges were you facing before Rottay?
2. What were you using before? Walk me through your previous stack.
3. What wasn't working about that approach?
4. Can you quantify the impact? (Time, cost, team frustration)
5. What was the breaking point that made you look for alternatives?
6. What other solutions did you consider?

### The Solution (10 minutes)

1. How did you first hear about Rottay?
2. What made you choose Rottay over alternatives?
3. Who was involved in the evaluation process?
4. What was the deciding factor?
5. How was the implementation process?
6. What was your timeline from decision to production?
7. Which modules do you use, and how?
8. What surprised you (positively or negatively)?

### The Results (10 minutes)

1. What's different now compared to before?
2. Can you quantify the improvements? (Cost, time, headcount)
3. How has this affected your team?
4. How has this affected your customers?
5. Have you achieved any certifications faster?
6. What would you have done with those engineering hours?

### The Quote (5 minutes)

1. How would you describe Rottay in one sentence?
2. What would you tell someone evaluating Rottay?
3. What's the biggest benefit you've seen?
4. Would you recommend Rottay? Why?

### Closing

1. Is there anything else you'd like to add?
2. Are there any metrics or outcomes we should follow up on?
3. Would you be open to being a reference for prospects?

## Best Practices

### Writing Tips

1. **Lead with outcomes** - Start with the result, then explain how they got there
2. **Be specific** - "60% cost reduction" is better than "significant savings"
3. **Use their words** - Authentic quotes are more compelling than polished ones
4. **Show, don't tell** - Include specific examples, not just claims
5. **Keep it scannable** - Use headers, bullets, and tables

### Quote Guidelines

- Get 3-5 quotes, use the best 2-3
- Ensure quotes are attributable (name + title)
- Prefer specific outcomes over general praise
- "Rottay saved us 20 hours per month" > "Rottay is great"

### Metrics to Capture

| Category | Metrics to Ask About |
|----------|---------------------|
| Time | Implementation time, time saved monthly, time to compliance |
| Cost | Vendor costs, engineering costs, total savings |
| Efficiency | Tickets reduced, incidents prevented, automation rate |
| Business | Revenue enabled, deals closed, expansion |

### Common Mistakes to Avoid

- Writing too much about Rottay, not enough about the customer
- Using jargon the customer didn't use
- Overstating outcomes (customers will push back)
- Skipping quantitative metrics
- Not getting proper approvals

## Approval Workflow

### Internal Review

1. Marketing reviews for messaging alignment
2. Product reviews for technical accuracy
3. Legal reviews for claims and disclosures
4. Sales reviews for usefulness

### Customer Review

1. Send draft to customer contact
2. Allow 5-7 business days for review
3. Address all feedback
4. Get explicit written approval
5. Confirm usage rights (web, sales, ads, etc.)

### Revision Process

- Minor edits: Inform customer, no re-approval needed
- Major edits: Require customer re-approval
- Metrics updates: Verify with customer before publishing
